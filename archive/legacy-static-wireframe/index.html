Why now

Malaysia has secured immense infrastructure commitments.
The challenge now is turning capacity into enterprise value.

With RM184.7 billion (US$91.8B equivalent) approved in data-centre-related investments since 2021 and 44% of 1H 2026 investment approvals concentrated in cloud and data centres, Malaysia's infrastructure build-out is moving fast. The decisions that matter now are practical: which workloads will run, how enterprises access compute, how power grids adapt, and how infrastructure investments become commercially viable.

The next phase of Malaysia's infrastructure story is not about announcing gigawatts. It is about connecting capacity to production AI.

Not an AI awareness conference. Not a data centre supply show.

AI InfraNext sits directly at the intersection: connecting the enterprise workloads creating demand with the infrastructure required to put AI into production.

Narrower than an enterprise AI summit

The question is not whether to adopt AI. It is what compute, networking, power, data architecture and security must exist beneath it.

Broader than a data centre event

Data centre capacity, power and cooling matter here because enterprise AI workloads depend on them, not as isolated real estate assets.

Built around Malaysian execution

Anchored in Malaysia's National AI Action Plan 2026–2030, domestic cloud regions, semiconductor engineering depth and enterprise production builds.

Programme

Four decisions. Two days.

Each pillar tackles an essential architectural decision required to bring AI into enterprise production.

01
Enterprise AI demand, compute and AI factories
Accelerated compute fabrics, workload profiling, and model deployment architectures
Coverage & Decisions
  • Enterprise AI workload profiling and capacity forecasting
  • GPU clusters, accelerated compute fabrics and interconnect topologies
  • AI factory architecture: training vs fine-tuning vs low-latency inference
  • Compute procurement economics: cloud, co-location and on-premise trade-offs
  • Hybrid deployment models for mission-critical enterprise systems
02
AI-ready data centres, power and thermal infrastructure
High-density racks, liquid cooling, grid access, and utility engineering
Coverage & Decisions
  • High-density rack deployments from 40kW to 100kW+ per rack
  • Direct-to-chip and immersion liquid cooling transition pathways
  • Grid interconnection, high-voltage sub-stations and TNB capacity planning
  • Renewable energy integration (Corporate Green Power Programme & TPA)
  • PUE and WUE efficiency compliance in tropical humid operating environments
03
Trusted cloud, data, connectivity and sovereign capability
National cloud policy, data residency, dark fibre, and cross-border governance
Coverage & Decisions
  • National Cloud Computing Policy (NCCP) implementation and compliance
  • Data residency, sovereignty and cross-border transfer rules under PDPA
  • Subsea cable landings, regional backhaul and terrestrial dark fibre
  • Sovereign AI models and Malaysian linguistic/cultural dataset governance
  • Multi-cloud interconnectivity and low-latency financial exchange fabrics
04
Secure and production-scale enterprise AI
Cyber Security Act 2024, model weight protection, zero trust, and reliability
Coverage & Decisions
  • Cyber Security Act 2024 compliance across Critical National Information Infrastructure
  • Securing AI model weights, training pipelines and fine-tuned enterprise checkpoints
  • Adversarial testing, data poisoning defense and prompt injection mitigation
  • Infrastructure identity, zero trust access for accelerated clusters and APIs
  • Operational resilience, failover engineering and high-availability architecture

Capital, policy, sustainability, infrastructure economics and talent run across all four.

Direct answers to core questions regarding participation, qualification and the programme.

AI InfraNext | Malaysia 2027 is a two-day executive summit connecting the enterprise workloads creating AI demand with the compute, cloud, data centre, power, connectivity, security and capital required for production AI.
The summit convenes five core constituencies: enterprise and public-sector workload owners (CIOs, CTOs, CISOs, CDAOs, heads of infrastructure), hyperscalers and AI cloud providers, data centre and utility operators, government policy leaders, and infrastructure project financiers.
It is narrower than a general AI summit because it ignores high-level awareness to focus on physical and architectural constraints. It is broader than a data centre event because facilities and power are evaluated specifically around the demands of enterprise AI workloads rather than as isolated real estate assets.
The programme is organized into four core pillars: 01 Enterprise AI demand, compute and AI factories; 02 AI-ready data centres, power and thermal infrastructure; 03 Trusted cloud, data, connectivity and sovereign capability; and 04 Secure and production-scale enterprise AI.
Senior enterprise, government and infrastructure leaders attend on a complimentary Delegate Pass by invitation and subject to steering committee qualification. Technology providers and consultancies can purchase a Business Pass for USD 499.
The summit takes place on 20–21 July 2027 in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia.
