About

From infrastructure capacity to enterprise value

Malaysia has approved tens of billions in data-centre and cloud investments. AI InfraNext exists for the executive decisions that connect that capacity to enterprise production.

A summit about the layer underneath enterprise AI

AI InfraNext | Malaysia 2027 is a two-day executive summit connecting the enterprises creating AI workload demand with the cloud, compute, data centre, power, connectivity, security and capital ecosystem required to run AI at scale.

Infrastructure on this scale is not decided in silos. It is decided continuously across power availability, thermal architecture, workload portability, sovereign data controls and deployment economics. AI InfraNext convenes that specific leadership group: enterprise workload owners, infrastructure operators, cloud platforms, national regulators and the capital underwriting the build.

Infrastructure is now the constraint, not the ambition

As Malaysian enterprises move from pilot projects to operational production, physical and digital infrastructure becomes the binding constraint on AI deployment. Production AI demands high-density accelerated compute, specialised cooling, grid stability, high-bandwidth interconnectivity, robust data governance and resilient cyber architectures.

This reality elevates a distinct executive group: heads of infrastructure, enterprise architects, cloud leaders, CISOs, data centre operators, power utility planners and infrastructure financiers. These roles are rarely the focus of general AI business conferences, yet they determine whether enterprise AI can operate reliably, securely and economically.

Market Foundation

Why Malaysia, why now

Four forces are converging simultaneously: institutionalised national AI policy, hyperscale digital infrastructure approvals, severe power and cooling requirements, and a deep industrial and semiconductor demand base.

4.1 Policy Execution

Policy is moving from ambition to execution

With the establishment of AI Malaysia Berhad as the national lead agency and the publication of the National AI Action Plan 2026–2030, Malaysia's policy framework has shifted toward concrete implementation. Supported by the National Cloud Computing Policy (NCCP) and the Cyber Security Act 2024, governance is being translated into clear operational and procurement rules.

4.2 Capital Deployment

Capital and capacity are moving into construction

According to MIDA, Malaysia approved approximately RM184.7 billion (US$91.8B equivalent) in data-centre-related investments from 2021 through 1H 2026. In the first half of 2026 alone, data-centre and cloud-computing projects accounted for US$22.8 billion equivalent — representing close to 44% of all approved investments across all sectors.

4.3 Physical Constraints

Power and cooling have become strategic priorities

At year-end 2025, Tenaga Nasional Berhad (TNB) recorded approximately 850MW of actual connected data centre load utilisation, with 35 projects representing roughly 4.5GW of total maximum demand in the connected pipeline. Securing high-density power supply, expanding renewable energy access, and deploying liquid cooling have become boardroom-level decisions.

4.4 Industrial Demand

Industrial and semiconductor depth creates real demand

Malaysia's deep semiconductor, packaging and electronics manufacturing base provides an immediate pool of compute-intensive, real-world AI workloads. Under the National Semiconductor Strategy and smart-factory initiatives targeting 3,000 smart facilities by 2030, production-scale AI is an industrial imperative rather than a theoretical exercise.

Malaysia has established the pipeline. The focus must now turn to deployment economics, workload execution and commercial value creation.

The distinction we are drawing

AI InfraNext enforces strict editorial boundaries to preserve actionable executive value.

Not a general AI adoption conference

The issue is not whether enterprises should use AI. It is what infrastructure must be engineered, powered and secured to deploy it in production.

Not a data centre real-estate showcase

Facilities, power and cooling are evaluated strictly as they relate to AI workload density and enterprise demand, not as passive property assets.

Not a policy advocacy lobby

Government participate as vital ecosystem partners. The summit does not lobby for specific commercial regulatory outcomes.

Not an uncapped vendor exhibition

Editorial control of the agenda sits strictly with Trescon. Sponsor speaking positions are capped to protect agenda independence.

Not a general consumer crowd

Delegate qualification is strictly enforced. Verified executive relevance is the only metric.

Pillars

Programme architecture

Four decisions spanning the entire infrastructure stack, running across two intensive days.

01
Enterprise AI demand, compute and AI factories
Workload profiling, GPU clustering, and model inference economics
  • Enterprise AI workload profiling and capacity forecasting
  • GPU clusters, accelerated compute fabrics and interconnect topologies
  • AI factory architecture: training vs fine-tuning vs low-latency inference
  • Compute procurement economics: cloud, co-location and on-premise trade-offs
  • Hybrid deployment models for mission-critical enterprise systems
02
AI-ready data centres, power and thermal infrastructure
High-density racks, liquid cooling, grid access, and utility engineering
  • High-density rack deployments from 40kW to 100kW+ per rack
  • Direct-to-chip and immersion liquid cooling transition pathways
  • Grid interconnection, high-voltage sub-stations and TNB capacity planning
  • Renewable energy integration (Corporate Green Power Programme & TPA)
  • PUE and WUE efficiency compliance in tropical humid operating environments
03
Trusted cloud, data, connectivity and sovereign capability
National cloud policy, data residency, dark fibre, and cross-border governance
  • National Cloud Computing Policy (NCCP) implementation and compliance
  • Data residency, sovereignty and cross-border transfer rules under PDPA
  • Subsea cable landings, regional backhaul and terrestrial dark fibre
  • Sovereign AI models and Malaysian linguistic/cultural dataset governance
  • Multi-cloud interconnectivity and low-latency financial exchange fabrics
04
Secure and production-scale enterprise AI
Cyber Security Act 2024, model weight protection, zero trust, and reliability
  • Cyber Security Act 2024 compliance across Critical National Information Infrastructure
  • Securing AI model weights, training pipelines and fine-tuned enterprise checkpoints
  • Adversarial testing, data poisoning defense and prompt injection mitigation
  • Infrastructure identity, zero trust access for accelerated clusters and APIs
  • Operational resilience, failover engineering and high-availability architecture

Capital, policy, sustainability, infrastructure economics and talent run across all four.
